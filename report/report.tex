\begin{abstract}
This report solves the B737--800 cruise-phase flight-planning problem
from the project statement under the prescribed polynomial wind field.
Two methods are implemented and compared on the same six-state aircraft
model: a Hermite--Simpson direct-collocation transcription solved with
IPOPT, and a single closed-loop policy trained by multi-flight
behavioural cloning on a manifold of direct-collocation experts plus
per-flight kNN DAgger correction, all wrapped in a Gymnasium environment
with a deterministic terminal-guidance layer. The direct solution is
used as the reference because it enforces the destination exactly. The
policy method is evaluated as a practical feedback controller: a single
unified checkpoint flies all three test scenarios, following the learned
policy over the cruise segment and switching to the terminal-guidance
layer only inside the final approach corridor to close the waypoint and
altitude errors. All three prescribed flights are solved, and all eight
required plots are reported for each flight.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The project asks for optimized trajectories for three B737--800 cruise
flights under a known wind field. The required state is
$\mathbf{x}=(x_e,y_n,h,V,\psi,m)^\top$, and the controls are
$\mathbf{u}=(\gamma,\mu,\delta)^\top$, namely flight-path angle, bank
angle, and throttle. The two submitted methods are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Direct collocation}: Hermite--Simpson with IPOPT/MUMPS.
  \item \textbf{Closed-loop policy}: a Stable-Baselines3 PPO actor
  network trained by multi-expert behavioural cloning and DAgger-style
  correction, evaluated with a terminal-guidance safety layer.
\end{itemize}
Dynamic programming is not included in the final submission. The final
comparison is kept between methods that operate on the full six-state
aircraft model.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\subsection{Dynamics}
The dynamics are integrated in a flat Earth East--North--Up frame
anchored at each flight's initial longitude and latitude. The wind
polynomial is evaluated in longitude/latitude degrees after converting
the current ENU state back to $(\lambda,\varphi)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot{x}_e &= V\cos\psi\cos\gamma + W_x(\lambda,\varphi),\\
\dot{y}_n &= V\sin\psi\cos\gamma + W_y(\lambda,\varphi),\\
\dot{h}   &= V\sin\gamma,\\
\dot{V}   &= \frac{T-D}{m} - g_0\sin\gamma,\\
\dot{\psi}&= \frac{C_L S\rho V}{2m}\frac{\sin\mu}{\cos\gamma},\\
\dot{m}   &= -f(V,h,\delta).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dyn}
\end{equation}
Because the PDF equations use $\dot{x}=V\cos\psi$ and
$\dot{y}=V\sin\psi$, $\psi$ is measured counter-clockwise from east:
$\psi=0$ points east and $\psi=\pi/2$ points north. This convention is
used consistently in the code, plots, and tables.

\subsection{Aircraft and Wind Models}
The density model is
$\rho(h)=\rho_0(1-2.2257\times10^{-5}h)^{4.2586}$. Lift is computed
from the coordinated-turn balance
$C_L=2mg/(\rho S V^2\cos\mu)$, the drag polar is
$C_D=C_{D0}+kC_L^2$, and $D=\tfrac12\rho V^2SC_D$. Maximum thrust is
\[
T_{\max}=C_{T,\mathrm{cr}}C_{T,c1}
\left(1-\frac{h_{\mathrm{ft}}}{C_{T,c2}}
      +C_{T,c3}h_{\mathrm{ft}}^2\right),
\]
with $T=\delta T_{\max}$. Fuel flow is
$f=\delta T_{\max}\eta C_{f,\mathrm{cr}}$ and
$\eta=(C_{f1}/60000)(1+V_{\mathrm{kt}}/C_{f2})$.

The wind field is the project Table 2 polynomial
\[
W_{\{x,y\}}=c_0+c_1\lambda+c_2\varphi+c_3\lambda\varphi
+c_4\lambda^2+c_5\varphi^2+c_6\lambda^2\varphi
+c_7\lambda\varphi^2+c_8\lambda^2\varphi^2.
\]
All calculations are SI internally; longitude/latitude degrees are used
only for wind lookup and plotting context.

\subsection{Objective and Bounds}
The PDF objective is
\begin{equation}
J=(x(t_f)-x_d)^2+(y(t_f)-y_d)^2+(h(t_f)-h_d)^2
  +\int_0^{t_f}(0.05+f)\,dt .
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
The direct method enforces $(x(t_f),y(t_f),h(t_f))=(x_d,y_d,h_d)$ as
hard equalities and therefore minimizes only the running part of
\eqref{eq:cost}. The policy method is accepted when it enters a terminal
disk of 500\,m horizontal error and 300\,m altitude error; its reported
cost still includes the squared terminal error, so the policy cost
column is not directly comparable to the direct cost column.

The bounds used in both methods are
$h\in[0,13000]$ m, $V\in[120,270]$ m/s,
$m\in[m_{\mathrm{OEW}},m_0]$,
$\gamma\in[-6^\circ,10^\circ]$, $\mu\in[-30^\circ,30^\circ]$,
$\delta\in[0.05,1]$, and $t_f\in[600,18000]$ s.

\section{Method A: Direct Collocation}
The direct method uses a compressed Hermite--Simpson transcription on a
uniform grid. Time is normalized to $\tau\in[0,1]$ and $t_f$ is a
decision variable. For segment $k$,
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{u}_{kc} &= \tfrac12(\mathbf{u}_k+\mathbf{u}_{k+1}),\\
\mathbf{x}_{kc} &= \tfrac12(\mathbf{x}_k+\mathbf{x}_{k+1})
 +\tfrac{\Delta t}{8}(f_k-f_{k+1}),\\
\mathbf{0} &= \mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_k
 -\tfrac{\Delta t}{6}(f_k+4f_{kc}+f_{k+1}).
\end{align*}
The running cost is integrated with Simpson's rule on the same mesh.
The state variables are nondimensionalized before IPOPT sees them:
$(x,y)/10^5$, $h/10^4$, $V/250$, $m/10^5$, and $t/3600$.

The initial guess is a straight ENU line from the initial waypoint to
the destination with linearly interpolated altitude and mass. IPOPT is
run with MUMPS, $\mathrm{tol}=10^{-6}$, adaptive barrier strategy,
limited-memory Hessian, and a 3000-iteration cap. Acceptable-termination
thresholds are enabled --- $\mathtt{acceptable\_tol}=10^{-4}$,
$\mathtt{acceptable\_iter}=15$,
$\mathtt{acceptable\_constr\_viol\_tol}=10^{-6}$ --- so an iterate that
has been feasible to $\sim10^{-9}$ and stalled on the optimal-cost
plateau for several iterations is accepted, instead of being driven into
\texttt{Error\_In\_Step\_Computation} when restoration cannot be entered.
Without this softer criterion the Flight-2 cold solve, which finds the
lower-cost feasible local optimum at iteration $\sim100$, is rejected and the next-best
warm-start candidate (a bang-bang local optimum) is promoted by the
candidate-selection rule.
A warm-start from Flight 1 is also tested for Flights 2 and 3, but it is
treated only as an additional candidate. The canonical direct result is
the converged candidate with the lowest PDF cost. This matters: for all
three flights, the cold-start solution has lower cost than the
Flight-1 warm-start local optimum.

\section{Method B: Closed-Loop Policy}
The policy method is implemented as a custom Gymnasium environment
wrapping the same dynamics as \eqref{eq:dyn}. The action is normalized
to $[-1,1]^3$ and mapped to $(\gamma,\mu,\delta)$ bounds. The
observation is a 45-dimensional vector containing destination error in
ENU, altitude error, speed and mass fractions, $\sin\psi$ and $\cos\psi$,
local wind, time fraction, distance fraction, a one-hot flight-id
indicator, and the previous actions. The integration step is
$\Delta t = 1$\,s and the episode horizon is $3\times10^{4}$ steps, which
covers the longest of the three flights.

\paragraph{One policy, three flights.}
The course rule requires one trained policy that flies all three
scenarios. The environment therefore samples a flight id at every
\texttt{reset} when the caller passes \texttt{flight\_id="random"} and
keeps the same disk/altitude tolerance for every flight, so a single
checkpoint is evaluated on F1, F2, and F3 in turn. Per-flight policies
are not produced.

The actor network is the Stable-Baselines3 PPO MLP architecture. The
unified policy is trained in four stages: a multi-flight imitation
manifold from direct-collocation experts, behavioural cloning across
nine seeds, per-flight kNN DAgger refinement, and finally on-policy PPO
fine-tuning. Each stage carries a best-of guard against regression, so
the final checkpoint is at worst as good as the previous stage.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Multi-flight, multi-expert dataset.} For each of the three
  flights the direct solver is run from the nominal initial state and
  from a per-flight perturbation ball. Flight 2 and Flight 3 use a
  uniform half-width of $\pm0.5^\circ$ in $\lambda$ and $\varphi$,
  $\pm200$\,m in $h$, $\pm10$\,m/s in $V$, and $\pm8^\circ$ in $\psi$.
  Flight 1 has the longest horizon and the weakest observed
  out-of-training robustness, so its ball is widened to
  $\pm1.0^\circ$ in $\lambda$ and $\varphi$, $\pm300$\,m in $h$,
  $\pm15$\,m/s in $V$, and $\pm15^\circ$ in $\psi$, and the number of
  perturbed solves is doubled. When a cold guess fails the Flight-1
  nominal solution is piped in as a warm start. The combined manifold
  is on the order of 25 expert trajectories.
  \item \textbf{Mixed-flight BC seed search.} Nine BC seeds are trained
  on the mixed dataset for 400 epochs each. Every candidate is evaluated
  on all three flights, and the seed with the lowest aggregate score
  (sum of fuel plus a large penalty whenever a flight does not reach
  the disk) is kept as the BC baseline.
  \item \textbf{Per-flight kNN DAgger~\cite{dagger}.} The BC baseline is then rolled
  out on each flight in turn; the visited states are queried against
  per-flight kNN labelers built from the expert manifold, the
  highest-disagreement transitions are kept, and the aggregated data is
  retrained. A best-of-rounds criterion against the BC baseline guards
  against regression.
  \item \textbf{On-policy PPO fine-tuning~\cite{ppo}.} The
  imitation checkpoint is then continued as the warm start of a PPO
  run on a $4\times$-vectorized mixed-flight env (each parallel env
  draws a fresh flight id on every \texttt{reset}). The actor's
  initial log-std is shrunk to $-2.5$ so the BC mean is not erased
  in the first updates, and every step receives an expert-anchor
  bonus $-\lambda_a\,d_{\mathrm{emb}}^2$ ($\lambda_a=0.3$) that
  mildly discourages drift off the expert manifold. After
  600\,000 environment steps the candidate is scored on all three
  flights; the PPO checkpoint is only accepted if it strictly
  improves the disk-reach count or matches it with lower aggregate
  fuel, otherwise the imitation checkpoint is kept. This stage gives
  the network an opportunity to do genuine on-policy reward shaping
  while keeping the imitation prior as a hard floor.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Terminal guidance.}
The learned policy is responsible for the cruise segment, the descent
initiation, and the final-turn alignment. Only inside the last 20\,km
to the waypoint does a deterministic guidance layer take over with a
proportional heading command toward the waypoint, a line-of-sight
flight-path-angle command toward the target altitude, and a small
exponential moving average on the gamma command so the layer does not
introduce sample-to-sample chatter. This layer is not an optimizer
and does not use the direct solution; it is a thin safety layer that
converts the learned cruise policy into a reliable closed-loop arrival
controller over the final $\sim100$\,s of flight. The acceptance disk
is 500\,m horizontal and 300\,m vertical; tighter disks were tested but
they are unreachable at $\Delta t=1$\,s without a phased mesh because a
decelerating aircraft at $\sim130$\,m/s can skip over a $<200$\,m disk
between two integration samples. An earlier version of this work used
a 100\,km switch radius; the present 20\,km radius shrinks the
hand-coded portion of the trajectory from $\sim10$\,minutes to
$\sim100$\,s and forces the learned policy to do the work of choosing
when and how steeply to descend.

Direct collocation reaches the destination exactly by construction and
is the numerical reference. The single unified policy enters the
terminal disk on all three nominal flights. Its fuel burns are close to
but consistently above the direct reference --- $+4.6\%$ on Flight 1,
$+2.7\%$ on Flight 2, and $+1.7\%$ on Flight 3. The direct method
therefore remains the tightest local-optimal benchmark,
and the policy is best understood as a feedback controller whose
single-shot rollout approximates the open-loop optimum to a few
percent.

An earlier version of this comparison reported that the policy beat
the direct solver on Flight 2. That conclusion was an artifact of
IPOPT terminating Flight-2's cold-started NLP with
``Error\_In\_Step\_Computation`` at iteration 1010 even though the
constraint violation was below $10^{-9}$. The candidate-selection rule
then promoted the Flight-1-warm-started solution, which had landed on
a bang-bang local optimum with a 5-cycle altitude-and-throttle limit
cycle (\,$\pm1000$\,m altitude swings,
$\delta\in\{0.05,1.0\}$ chatter). Enabling IPOPT's
\texttt{acceptable\_tol} $=10^{-4}$ together with
\texttt{acceptable\_iter} $=15$ allows the cold solve to terminate with
\texttt{Solved\_To\_Acceptable\_Level}, yielding the smooth Flight-2
trajectory reported here. The unscaled NLP error of that acceptable
iterate is $\approx4\times10^{-3}$, three orders of magnitude tighter
than the bang-bang basin's $\approx2$.

\subsection{Flight-by-Flight Observations}
Flight 1 is the long east-northeast route. Both methods climb toward the
upper cruise envelope and descend near the destination. The policy
follows a slightly slower-but-shorter wind-aware track and closes the
final disk with the terminal-guidance layer; the resulting fuel is
$+4.6\%$ above the direct reference.

Flight 2 is the sharp-turn case starting at $\psi_0=225^\circ$. The
direct solution climbs aggressively to the cruise ceiling at 13\,000\,m
and stays there until a final clean descent into the terminal disk; its
throttle stays at 1.0 during the climb, settles to $\approx0.95$ during
the ceiling cruise (where the altitude bound is active), and drops to
0.05 for the descent. The policy follows the same qualitative profile:
climb-cruise-descent at 13\,000\,m, arriving 0.8 minute earlier and
burning 2.7\% more fuel than the direct solver. The terminal guidance
closes the final 500\,m disk; no zero-order-hold imitation repair is
needed because the BC training data is sampled on the same
$\Delta t=1$\,s grid that the policy uses at execution.

Flight 3 is mostly northbound and has the largest altitude drop at the
destination. The learned policy keeps the long cruise segment close to
the direct solution; the terminal guidance is most visible here because
it forces the final descent into the 7000\,m target altitude.

\subsection{Out-of-Training Robustness}

conditions per flight, with standard deviations slightly larger than the
training half-widths ($\sigma_\lambda=\sigma_\varphi=0.7^\circ$,
$\sigma_h=300$\,m, $\sigma_V=12$\,m/s, $\sigma_\psi=10^\circ$) so a
fraction of test ICs fall outside the BC training ball. The same
terminal guidance layer was active. Results are summarized in

The weakest robustness case is Flight 1, the long east-northeast route.
This is consistent with the structure of the training data: F1 also has
the longest horizon (about 13\,150\,s at $\Delta t=1$\,s, an order of
magnitude more decision steps than F2), so a perturbed initial state
accumulates a much larger heading drift before reaching the destination
than on F2 or F3. The policy does not fail catastrophically on F1
robustness trials --- it still flies a smooth trajectory --- but the
accumulated track error frequently puts the destination outside the
500\,m disk by the time the policy enters the terminal corridor. F2 and
F3 retain exactly $60\%$ disk-reach on out-of-training Gaussians and
median closest-xy under 500\,m. Improving F1 robustness without re-training
would require either widening the BC perturbation ball on F1 only or
adding a cruise-phase outer loop on track error; both are reserved for
future work.

\section{Conclusions}
The direct collocation method provides the tightest cost reference here:
it satisfies the terminal waypoint exactly and yields locally optimal
trajectories for the PDF objective on a 241-node mesh. We do not claim
global optimality --- IPOPT only certifies first-order KKT conditions
on a nonconvex NLP --- but the candidate-selection rule, which compares
cold and warm-started solutions and keeps the lower-cost converged
candidate, makes accidental homotopy traps far less likely. Two engineering details that turned out to be
necessary in practice are documented above:
(i) enabling IPOPT's acceptable-termination thresholds so that the
Flight-2 cold solve can exit cleanly at its lower-cost local optimum rather than
chase $\mathrm{tol}=10^{-6}$ on an already-feasible iterate, and
(ii) keeping both the cold start and the Flight-1 warm start as
parallel candidates and selecting on lowest cost --- for all three
flights, the cold-start solution beats the warm-start local optimum,
sometimes by 8--19\% in fuel.
The closed-loop policy method is less exact but operationally useful: a
single unified checkpoint trained on the multi-flight imitation manifold
and refined first by per-flight kNN DAgger and then by on-policy PPO
fine-tuning reaches the 500\,m terminal disk on all three nominal
flights. The policy is responsible for cruise, descent initiation, and
final-turn alignment; only the last $\sim100$\,s inside a 20\,km
corridor are handled by a thin deterministic terminal-guidance layer.
The policy's main limitation is that it satisfies a finite arrival disk
rather than exact endpoint equality, which is why the direct method
remains the benchmark for the optimal-control cost. The best-of guards
at every imitation and RL stage ensure that the deployed checkpoint is
never worse than its predecessors and let us claim that PPO's
contribution, when any, is a strict improvement.
