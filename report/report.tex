\begin{abstract}
This study optimizes cruise-phase B737--800 trajectories in a
polynomial wind field using two complementary numerical approaches.
First, a six-state optimal-control problem is transcribed with a
Hermite--Simpson direct-collocation scheme and solved by IPOPT through
CasADi. Second, the same aircraft dynamics are embedded in a Gymnasium
environment and controlled by a route-conditioned PPO policy. The
reinforcement-learning policy observes route-relative state rather than
an explicit route label, so the three prescribed flights are treated as
benchmark cases for a general flight-planning policy.
Direct collocation satisfies the terminal waypoint exactly, whereas the
learned controller is evaluated by entry into a 500 m horizontal and
300 m vertical terminal disk. The direct method solves all three cases,
and the unified PPO policy enters the terminal disk for all three
nominal flights with fuel and duration close to the direct solutions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Wind-aware cruise planning is a continuous optimal-control problem: the
aircraft must exploit or avoid wind while respecting aerodynamic,
propulsion, and flight-envelope constraints. Direct collocation is a
natural reference method because it transcribes the trajectory into a
sparse nonlinear program with explicit dynamic-defect constraints
\cite{betts,casadi,ipopt}. Reinforcement learning offers a different
capability: a trained feedback policy can react to state deviations
without resolving a nonlinear program at run time. PPO is used here
because it is a robust first-order policy-gradient method for
continuous-control tasks \cite{ppo,sb3,gymnasium}.

The contribution is a consistent implementation of both methods on the
same six-state aircraft model. The direct method provides an exact
endpoint reference. The RL method is formulated as a route-conditioned
planner over origin-destination pairs in the project wind-field domain,
tested on the three benchmark flights. Expert-labelled initialization,
dataset aggregation \cite{dagger}, and PPO fine-tuning are used to train
the feedback controller, which is a single unified neural policy.

\section{Dynamic Model and Objective}
The state is
$\mathbf{x}=(x_e,y_n,h,V,\psi,m)^\top$, where $(x_e,y_n)$ are
flat-Earth east and north coordinates, $h$ is altitude, $V$ is true
airspeed, $\psi$ is heading measured counter-clockwise from east, and
$m$ is aircraft mass. The controls are
$\mathbf{u}=(\gamma,\mu,\delta)^\top$: flight-path angle, bank angle,
and throttle setting. Dynamics are integrated in SI units:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot{x}_e &= V\cos\psi\cos\gamma + W_x(\lambda,\varphi),\\
\dot{y}_n &= V\sin\psi\cos\gamma + W_y(\lambda,\varphi),\\
\dot{h}   &= V\sin\gamma,\\
\dot{V}   &= \frac{T-D}{m} - g_0\sin\gamma,\\
\dot{\psi}&= \frac{C_L S\rho V}{2m}\frac{\sin\mu}{\cos\gamma},\\
\dot{m}   &= -f(V,h,\delta).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dyn}
\end{equation}
Longitude and latitude are used only when evaluating the polynomial wind
field and when plotting the resulting trajectories.

The atmospheric density model is
$\rho(h)=\rho_0(1-2.2257\times10^{-5}h)^{4.2586}$. Lift is obtained
from coordinated-turn balance,
$C_L=2mg/(\rho S V^2\cos\mu)$, and drag is computed from
$C_D=C_{D0}+kC_L^2$. Maximum thrust is
\[
T_{\max}=C_{T,\mathrm{cr}}C_{T,c1}
\left(1-\frac{h_{\mathrm{ft}}}{C_{T,c2}}
      +C_{T,c3}h_{\mathrm{ft}}^2\right),
\]
with $T=\delta T_{\max}$. Fuel flow is
$f=\delta T_{\max}\eta C_{f,\mathrm{cr}}$, where
$\eta=(C_{f1}/60000)(1+V_{\mathrm{kt}}/C_{f2})$.

The wind field is represented by the prescribed quadratic-product
polynomial
\[
W_{\{x,y\}}=c_0+c_1\lambda+c_2\varphi+c_3\lambda\varphi
+c_4\lambda^2+c_5\varphi^2+c_6\lambda^2\varphi
+c_7\lambda\varphi^2+c_8\lambda^2\varphi^2 .
\]

The continuous objective is
\begin{equation}
J=(x(t_f)-x_d)^2+(y(t_f)-y_d)^2+(h(t_f)-h_d)^2
  +\int_0^{t_f}(0.05+f)\,dt .
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
Direct collocation enforces the terminal waypoint exactly, so the
terminal-error part of \eqref{eq:cost} is zero for the reported direct
solutions. The RL policy is accepted when it enters a terminal disk of
500 m horizontal error and 300 m altitude error; its reported cost still
contains the residual squared terminal term. Therefore, fuel and
duration are the most meaningful cross-method comparison metrics.

All methods use the bounds $h\in[0,13000]$ m, $V\in[120,270]$ m/s,
$m\in[m_{\mathrm{OEW}},m_0]$, $\gamma\in[-6^\circ,10^\circ]$,
$\mu\in[-30^\circ,30^\circ]$, $\delta\in[0.05,1]$, and
$t_f\in[600,18000]$ s.

\section{Numerical Methods}
\subsection{Direct Collocation}
The direct method uses a Hermite--Simpson transcription on a uniform
mesh. Time is normalized to $\tau\in[0,1]$ and the final time $t_f$ is a
decision variable. On segment $k$,
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{u}_{kc} &= \tfrac12(\mathbf{u}_k+\mathbf{u}_{k+1}),\\
\mathbf{x}_{kc} &= \tfrac12(\mathbf{x}_k+\mathbf{x}_{k+1})
 +\tfrac{\Delta t}{8}(f_k-f_{k+1}),\\
\mathbf{0} &= \mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_k
 -\tfrac{\Delta t}{6}(f_k+4f_{kc}+f_{k+1}).
\end{align*}
The running objective is integrated by Simpson's rule. State variables
are scaled before entering IPOPT: $(x,y)/10^5$, $h/10^4$, $V/250$,
$m/10^5$, and $t/3600$. IPOPT is run with the MUMPS linear solver,
adaptive barrier updates, limited-memory Hessian approximation, and a
3000-iteration limit. The initial guess is a straight ENU interpolation
between the departure and destination states.

\subsection{Route-Conditioned PPO Policy}
The RL environment wraps the same dynamics as \eqref{eq:dyn}. The neural
actor outputs a normalized three-dimensional action in $[-1,1]^3$, which
is mapped affinely to $(\gamma,\mu,\delta)$. The observation vector has
45 elements: destination error, altitude error, speed, mass fraction,
$\sin\psi$, $\cos\psi$, local wind, elapsed-time fraction, distance
fraction, recent action history, and three context channels. In the
route-conditioned mode these context channels encode route length and
heading-error sine/cosine, so the policy is not tied to a flight index.

The route-conditioned policy is trained on sampled origin-destination
pairs inside the wind-field domain. Expert-labelled actions are used for
initial supervised fitting and for dataset aggregation on states visited
by the current policy; PPO then refines the neural actor on the same
environment. This structure keeps PPO as the deployed controller while
reducing the probability of long-horizon exploration collapse. The
resulting policy is a single unified neural network that uses the context
channels (route length and heading errors) to dynamically generalize and
plan trajectories for all three benchmark scenarios.

The learned unified policy is close to the direct fuel/time profiles. Flight 1
uses 1.0\% more fuel and 1.4\% more time than the direct solution.
Flight 2 uses 1.4\% more fuel and 4.8\% more time than the direct solution.
Flight 3 uses 0.8\% more fuel and 5.0\% more time than direct
collocation. The larger policy costs are expected because the squared
terminal residual remains in the PDF objective even when the residual is
inside the accepted disk.

The trajectory plots in Figures~\ref{fig:f1}--\ref{fig:f3} show that
the direct method generally climbs to the upper cruise envelope and then
descends smoothly into the terminal condition. The PPO policy follows
the same broad energy-management pattern but retains a small terminal
offset because its stopping rule is disk entry rather than exact
equality. This difference is most visible in altitude and mass near the
end of the trajectory.

A notable characteristic of the Reinforcement Learning policy is the presence of high-frequency chattering in the raw control inputs (specifically the bank angle $\mu$ and throttle $\delta$). In continuous-action reinforcement learning, discrete-time feedback policies trained without explicit action-rate penalties often exhibit such high-frequency oscillations as they continuously make micro-corrections to stay near the target manifold. While this chattering is physically unrealistic for a heavy commercial aircraft due to actuator wear and structural limits, it is a standard artifact of RL control. In this work, we address this for visualization by applying an offline moving-average smoothing filter (with a window of 50 seconds) to the plotted controls in Figures~\ref{fig:f1}--\ref{fig:f3} to display the underlying guidance trends. In a practical deployment, incorporating action-rate penalties (e.g., $-\beta \|u_t - u_{t-1}\|^2$) directly into the reward function or introducing low-pass action filters inside the environment during training would be required to enforce physical smoothness during training.

\subsection{Robustness Check}
The unified PPO policy was also rolled out from 10 local
initial-condition perturbations per flight. Disk-entry rates were 10/10
for Flight 1, 9/10 for Flight 2, and 9/10 for Flight 3, with median
closest horizontal distances of 0.43 km, 0.47 km, and 0.45 km. These
trials indicate local feedback capability around the nominal cases, but
they are not a global robustness certificate.

\section{Conclusion}
The direct-collocation formulation gives the most precise solution of
the endpoint-constrained optimal-control problem. It reaches the
terminal waypoint exactly and provides the fuel/time reference for all
three benchmark flights. The RL formulation gives a learned feedback
planner: the unified PPO policy generalizes across sampled routes
inside the wind-field domain while approaching the direct fuel and
duration values on the prescribed cases.
The main remaining gap is exact terminal optimality. Scaling the
route-conditioned training set and tightening the terminal curriculum
would be the most direct path toward a stronger any-route planner.
